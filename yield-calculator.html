What Do These Results Mean?

Total Reaction Yield — Ytotal

Ytotal = σtotal · Nbeam · Ntarget

The total number of nuclear reactions actually occurring in the target over the whole irradiation — i.e. how many product particles/nuclei are physically created, regardless of whether anything detects them. It depends only on the beam and target: Nbeam = Q/(q·e) is the number of incident projectiles (with Q = I·t), and Ntarget = ρ·NA·(f%/100)/A is the number of target atoms per cm² seen by the beam. This is a "4π" quantity — it does not depend on detector geometry or efficiency at all.

Expected Detected Counts — Ndet

Ndet = Ytotal · (Ω/4π) · ε

Of those Ytotal reactions, only the fraction flying into the detector's solid angle Ω can be seen, and of those, only the fraction ε is actually registered (full-energy peak efficiency). This calculation assumes the reaction products are emitted isotropically, so the fraction reaching the detector is simply Ω/4π. This is the number of counts you'd expect in the spectrum's peak — the inverse of the cross-section calculator, where measured counts are used to extract σ instead.

Average Yield Rate — Ẏ

Ẏ = Ytotal / t

The total yield divided by the irradiation time — reactions per second, averaged over the whole run. Useful for estimating count rates, dead-time effects, or how long a run needs to be to accumulate a target number of reactions/counts. It is a rate of total reactions (not detected counts), assuming a constant beam current over the exposure.

1 barn = 10−²&sup4; cm²  ·  NA = 6.022 × 1023 mol−1  ·  e = 1.6 × 10−19 C
Uncertainties propagated in quadrature: (δY/Y)² = (δσ/σ)² + (δI/I)² + (δt/t)² + (δρ/ρ)² + (δf/f)²; detected counts additionally include (δΩ/Ω)² + (δε/ε)²
